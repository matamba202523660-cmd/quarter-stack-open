Safe Use of a Generator

This guide is for anyone operating or working near a portable generator. Follow these instructions whenever you need backup electricity, especially during a power outage.

What You Need

  • A generator in good working condition.
  • The manufacturer's instruction manual.
  • A dry, open-air location away from doors and windows.
  • Heavy-duty outdoor-rated extension cords in good condition.
  • A qualified electrician for connecting the generator to building wiring.

Steps

  1. Place the generator outdoors. Keep it at least 20 feet (about 6 metres) from buildings, doors, and windows, with the exhaust directed away from people and openings. Never operate it in a room, garage, or enclosed space.
  2. Check the generator before starting. Inspect the fuel lines, electrical cords, and fuel level. Turn the generator off and let it cool before refuelling.
  3. Keep fuel away from heat. Store petrol in an approved container, away from the generator and other ignition sources.
  4. Start it according to the manufacturer's instructions. Keep your hands dry and use suitable personal protective equipment (PPE) when required.
  5. Connect appliances safely. Use suitable outdoor-rated cords. Never plug the generator directly into a wall socket to power a building. This can cause dangerous backfeeding. Have a qualified electrician install an approved transfer switch.
  6. Manage the electrical load. Do not exceed the generator's rated capacity. Check its rating in kilovolt-amperes (kVA) and follow the manufacturer's limits.
  7. Shut down safely. Switch off connected appliances, turn off the generator as directed by its manual, and allow it to cool before storing or refuelling.

If Something Goes Wrong

If anyone develops a headache, dizziness, nausea, weakness, or confusion near a running generator, suspect carbon monoxide poisoning. Get everyone into fresh air immediately and call emergency services. Do not re-enter the affected area until emergency responders say it is safe.

If you notice sparks, damaged cords, fuel leaks, or an electrical fire, keep away, stop the generator only if it is safe to do so, and seek qualified assistance. Never refuse to act when someone may be in danger.
